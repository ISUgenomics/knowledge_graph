# Formation of Multilayer Cu Islands Embedded beneath the Surface of Graphite: Characterization and Fundamental Insights

## Abstract

We\npresent an extensive experimental study of the conditions under\nwhich Cu forms encapsulated islands under the top surface layers of\ngraphite, as a result of physical vapor deposition of Cu on argon-ion-bombarded\ngraphite. When the substrate is held at 800 K during deposition, conditions\nare optimal for formation of encapsulated multilayer Cu islands. Deposition\ntemperatures below 600 K favor adsorbed Cu clusters, while deposition\ntemperatures above 800 K favor a different type of feature that is\nprobably a single-layer intercalated Cu island. The multilayer Cu\nislands are characterized with respect to size and shape, thickness\nand continuity of the graphitic overlayer, relationship to graphite\nsteps, and stability in air. The experimental techniques are scanning\ntunneling microscopy and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy. We also\npresent an extensive study using density functional theory to compare\nstabilities of a wide variety of configurations of Cu atoms, Cu clusters,\nand Cu layers on/under the graphite surface. The only configuration\nthat is significantly more stable under the graphite surface than\non top of it, is a single Cu atom. This analysis leads us to conclude\nthat formation of encapsulated Cu islands is kinetically driven, rather\nthan thermodynamically driven.
