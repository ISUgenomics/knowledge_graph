# Residues2Revenues: Strengthening Producers, Products, and Profits through a Regenerative Feedmill

## Abstract

U.S. agriculture produces more than 400 million tons of crop residues each year, representing one of the nation’s largest untapped biological resources. Residues2Revenues (R2R) is a bold, transdisciplinary initiative designed to unlock new uses and expand markets for agricultural crop residues and strengthen the economic resilience of U.S. farms. Through a public-private partnership among universities, producers, and bioenergy industries, R2R addresses pressing challenges at the crop-livestock interface while conserving natural resources and supporting rural economic growth. Residues2Revenues will advance a next-generation residue-to-value system that expands the existing platform through five integrated goals: Goal 1: Develop market-driven conversion of corn stover residue in addition to winter-grown cereal rye and native grasses into feed and meal; Goal 2: Enable scientific breakthroughs toward the production of higher-value co-products including Sustainable Avion Fuel (SAF)-compatible blendstocks, biochar, and hydroxycinnamic acid (HCA) specialty chemicals; Goal 3: Optimize on-farm processes related to AD through integration of AI-tools, reducing operational costs and improving on-farm processes; Goal 4: Regenerate soil health and nutrient cycles through the application of AD co-products, and Goal 5: Build markets and workforce capacity to solve technological, management, policy, and regulatory challenges. Like Grass2Gas, R2R employs a transdisciplinary, systems approach to solving on-farm challenges and advancing pathways for rapid commercialization. This approach ensures that technological innovation is paired with economic feasibility, producer adoption, and scalable market development. By transforming crop residues from underutilized waste products into strategic agricultural resources, R2R will create new income streams for farmers, strengthen rural economies, reduce reliance on synthetic inputs, and position U.S. agriculture as a leader in the emerging bioeconomy.
