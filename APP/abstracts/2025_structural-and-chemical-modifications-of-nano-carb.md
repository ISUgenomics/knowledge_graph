# Structural and Chemical Modifications of Nano-Carbon Catalysts for Efficient Electrochemical Decarboxylation

## Abstract

Kolbe electrolysis presents a promising electrochemical strategy for upgrading biomass-derived carboxylic acids into biofuels and value-added chemicals by utilizing green electrons to drive decarboxylation and carbon-carbon coupling reactions. However, the efficiency and selectivity of this process are highly dependent on the properties of the electrode material. This study systematically investigates the electrochemical behavior of medium-chain fatty acids, particularly hexanoic acid, to elucidate the role of catalyst surface structure and chemistry in modulating reaction pathways. A comparative analysis of Kolbe electrolysis was performed using a diverse set of carbon-based materials, including carbon nanotubes (CNTs), carbon onions, activated carbon, and carbon black, to evaluate both catalytic and non-catalytic pathways. Among the tested materials, the in-lab synthesized CNTs exhibited the highest selectivity toward the Kolbe product, highlighting the importance of structural control and surface engineering. While CNTs demonstrated substantial promise as electrode materials, considerable variations in product distribution among different CNT sources emphasized the need for precise material design and optimization. The surface electron density of these materials was precisely tailored through heteroatom doping (N, B, O), which significantly influenced defect density, charge distribution, and catalytic activity. Nitrogen doping, in particular, enhanced electron density, leading to improved Kolbe selectivity by promoting controlled radical formation. On the other hand, boron and oxygen showed a lower Kolbe product selectivity. This indicates that the charge distribution, along with the surface polarity, impacts the product distribution. All these findings contribute to the fundamental understanding of structure-activity relationships in carbon-based electrodes and provide a tunable framework for the rational design of advanced carbon catalysts. By optimizing electrode materials, this work paves the way for more efficient and selective electrochemical transformations, expanding the potential of Kolbe electrolysis in sustainable chemical manufacturing and biofuel production.
