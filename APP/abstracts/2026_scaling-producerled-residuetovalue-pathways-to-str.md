# Scaling Producer‑Led Residue‑to‑Value Pathways to Strengthen U.S. Agricultural Competitiveness and Farm Income

## Abstract

Scaling Producer‑Led Residue‑to‑Value Pathways to Strengthen U.S. Agricultural Competitiveness and Farm Income (SPARV) is a transdisciplinary research, education, and extension project designed to develop producer‑led, scalable systems that convert underutilized agricultural residues, primarily corn stover, wheat straw, and sugarcane bagasse, into high‑value ruminant feed, nutrient‑efficient fertilizers, and renewable energy products. The project integrates sustainable residue management, biochemical and thermochemical conversion technologies, and coordinated supply‑chain analysis to improve farm profitability while maintaining soil health and long‑term agricultural productivity. Through five interconnected thrust areas, SPARV links residue processing and demineralization, animal feeding trials, fertilizer and biochar development, systems‑level techno‑economic and life‑cycle analysis, and coordinated extension and commercialization activities. SPARV will generate diverse datasets including laboratory measurements, field and animal trial data, techno‑economic and supply‑chain models, survey data, and education and extension products. These data will support decision‑support tools, regional deployment strategies, and commercialization pathways that enable economically viable residue utilization across diverse U.S. crop-livestock systems. The project emphasizes co‑design with producers, industry partners, and stakeholders to ensure that data products are relevant, reusable, and actionable, and that resulting residue‑to‑value systems strengthen domestic supply chains, enhance nutrient cycling, reduce reliance on external inputs, and support resilient rural economies.
