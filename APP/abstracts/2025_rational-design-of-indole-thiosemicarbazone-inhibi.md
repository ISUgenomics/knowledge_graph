# Rational Design of Indole Thiosemicarbazone Inhibitors for Enhanced Mild Steel Corrosion Inhibition

## Abstract

Corrosion inhibitors play a critical role in mitigating mild steel deterioration, a pervasive challenge in various industries. Aromatic molecules containing heteroatoms (N, O, S), such as pyrroles, pyridines, imidazoles, triazoles, thiazoles, and quinazolinones, have demonstrated notable inhibition efficiency against mild steel corrosion. The incorporation of functional side groups further enhances their performance. However, most studies rely on empirical, trial-and-error approaches, which are time-consuming and resource-intensive, limiting the systematic development of optimized inhibitors. In this study, we present a rational design strategy for corrosion inhibitors, utilizing indole-3-carboxaldehyde derivatives as a molecular platform. Indole-3-carboxaldehyde is a naturally occurring compound in human and animal digestive systems, formed from the bacterial degradation of L-tryptophan, a prevalent dietary amino acid. Instead of employing conventional heuristic methods, we systematically derivatized the indole structure to generate a library of structurally related molecules to establish structure-activity relationships (SAR) that can enhance inhibitor performance predictability. To further enhance predictive efficiency, we generated virtual molecular structures and quantum chemical parameters such as highest occupied molecular orbital energy (E HOMO ), lowest unoccupied molecular orbital energy (E LUMO ), energy gap (ΔE), dipole moment, and electron transfer number (ΔN) were computed for both synthesized and virtual molecules. These parameters provide insight into electronic properties and inhibition efficiency for prediction models, facilitating the determination of promising inhibitors. By generating structured datasets, this study lays the foundation for machine learning (ML)-guided models to predict inhibitor performance, offering a data-driven strategy to accelerate the identification of highly effective corrosion inhibitors. Overall, this study introduces a novel methodology for designing and evaluating corrosion inhibitors, leveraging a bio-based molecule. Our findings contribute to the growing efforts to replace traditional empirical methods with a systematic, predictive framework that enhances efficiency, and advances the understanding of corrosion inhibition mechanisms.
