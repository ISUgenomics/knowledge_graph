# Marker-Free Classification of Melanoma Cells by Machine Learning Following Their Isolation From Peripheral Blood by Dielectrophoresis

## Abstract

In this study, we present an image analysis and machine learning (ML) approach to distinguish melanoma cells from white blood cells (WBCs) without staining. This approach is applied in the context of the dielectrophoresis and bipolar electrode (DEP-BPE) platform, which isolates unlabeled, viable cells. A convolutional neural network (CNN) trained from scratch was compared to pre-trained models to evaluate discrimination between healthy donor and patient-derived cells, as well as between cells with or without DEP pre-selection. The CNNs achieved &gt;94% accuracy distinguishing melanoma cells from healthy donor WBCs, with EfficientNet-b0 demonstrating optimal performance with 96% sensitivity and minimal false positives. Additionally, models distinguished healthy donor WBCs from patient WBCs with &gt;96% accuracy, suggesting that healthy donor WBCs may not serve as appropriate models for patient-derived WBCs. This approach preserves cell viability and enables downstream single-cell functional assays.
